# Dividends and Corporate Actions

## Dividends

- A dividend is a cash payment to shareholders out of profits, declared per share (for example ₹10 per share).
- **Record date**: you must be on the company's register on this date to receive the dividend.
- With T+1 settlement, the **ex-date** is the same as the record date: buy before the ex-date to be eligible. On the ex-date the share price usually falls by about the dividend amount.
- Interim dividends are paid during the year; final dividends after annual results and shareholder approval.
- Dividends are taxed at your slab rate; see the tax guide.

## Bonus issue

Free additional shares in a ratio (for example 1:1 gives one extra share per share held). The share price adjusts down proportionally, so your total value is unchanged. It signals confidence and improves liquidity but creates no value by itself.

## Stock split

The face value is divided (for example ₹10 to ₹2, a 1:5 split). You get more shares at a proportionally lower price; total value is unchanged. Splits make shares more affordable for small investors.

## Buyback

The company buys its own shares, usually at a premium, through a tender offer or open-market purchases. It returns cash to shareholders and reduces the share count, raising EPS. A buyback at a high price can signal management believes the shares are undervalued.

## Rights issue

Existing shareholders are offered new shares, usually at a discount, in proportion to their holding. It raises capital for the company. If you do not subscribe (or sell your rights entitlement), your ownership is diluted.

## Mergers, demergers and delistings

- **Merger**: two companies combine; shareholders of the merged entity receive shares in the surviving company at a swap ratio (for example the HDFC Ltd–HDFC Bank merger in 2023).
- **Demerger**: a business is split into a separate listed company and shareholders receive its shares (for example Tata Motors splitting its passenger and commercial vehicle businesses in 2025, now listed as TMPV and TMCV).
- **Name or ticker changes** happen too: Zomato Ltd became Eternal Ltd (ticker ETERNAL) in 2025.
- **Delisting**: the promoter buys out public shareholders through a reverse book-building process.

## How SmartStock handles these

SmartStock uses current NSE tickers. Paper holdings are not adjusted for corporate actions automatically, so a split or bonus in a stock you hold virtually will show as a price drop.
