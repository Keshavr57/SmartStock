# Fundamental Analysis: Reading a Company

## The three statements

- **Profit and loss statement**: revenue, expenses and profit for a period (quarter or year).
- **Balance sheet**: what the company owns (assets) and owes (liabilities), and shareholders' equity, at a point in time.
- **Cash flow statement**: actual cash moving in and out from operations, investing and financing. Profit can be managed with accounting choices; cash is harder to fake.

Indian listed companies publish quarterly results within about 45 days of each quarter end (60 days for the March quarter, which includes annual results).

## What to look for

1. **Revenue growth** over 3 to 5 years, not one quarter. Check whether it comes from volume, price increases or acquisitions.
2. **Profit growth** that keeps pace with revenue, with stable or rising margins.
3. **Operating cash flow** close to net profit over several years. Persistently lower cash flow than profit can indicate aggressive accounting or rising receivables.
4. **Return ratios** (ROE, ROCE) consistently above about 15%.
5. **Debt**: falling or manageable debt and good interest coverage.
6. **Working capital**: rising receivable days or inventory days can signal trouble selling or collecting.

## Qualitative factors

- **Moat**: brand, scale, network effects, switching costs, licences or cost advantages that protect profits.
- **Management quality**: capital allocation, consistency of guidance, related-party transactions, treatment of minority shareholders.
- **Industry structure**: number of competitors, regulation, cyclicality.

## Ownership signals

- **Promoter holding**: a stable or rising stake signals confidence; steady selling is worth investigating.
- **Promoter pledging**: shares pledged as loan collateral. High or rising pledging is a red flag because a falling price can trigger forced selling.
- **FII and DII holding**: rising institutional ownership often follows improving fundamentals.

## Red flags

- Frequent changes of auditors or auditor resignations.
- Large related-party transactions.
- Profits growing while operating cash flow stays negative.
- Rising debt with falling return ratios.
- Repeated equity dilution through preferential allotments.
- Qualified audit opinions.

## Sector-specific metrics

- **Banks and NBFCs**: net interest margin (NIM), gross and net NPA, CASA ratio, capital adequacy, credit cost.
- **IT services**: constant-currency revenue growth, deal wins (TCV), attrition, EBIT margin.
- **FMCG**: volume growth, rural vs urban demand, gross margin (input costs).
- **Auto**: monthly sales volumes, EV transition, commodity costs.
- **Pharma**: US FDA inspection outcomes, US generics pricing, domestic formulations growth.
