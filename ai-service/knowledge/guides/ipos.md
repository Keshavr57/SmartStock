# IPOs in India

Rules as of 2025.

## What an IPO is

An initial public offering is when a company first sells shares to the public and lists on an exchange. Money from a **fresh issue** goes to the company; money from an **offer for sale (OFS)** goes to existing shareholders who are selling. A large OFS share means insiders are cashing out, which is worth understanding before applying.

## Book building, price band and lot size

Most mainboard IPOs use **book building**: the company announces a price band (for example ₹665 to ₹700), investors bid within it, and the final issue price is set after bidding closes. Retail investors can bid at the **cut-off price** (the final price, whatever it is).

Applications are made in **lots**. The minimum lot is sized so that one lot costs roughly ₹14,000 to ₹15,000 for mainboard issues.

## Investor categories and quotas (mainboard, book-built)

Each IPO reserves a fixed share (quota) of the issue for each category of investor:

- **QIB** (qualified institutional buyers such as mutual funds, banks, FPIs): up to 50% of the issue. Up to 60% of the QIB portion can go to **anchor investors** a day before the issue opens.
- **NII / HNI** (non-institutional investors applying above ₹2 lakh): at least 15%.
- **Retail individual investors (RII)** applying up to ₹2 lakh: at least 35% (the retail quota).
- Some issues reserve shares for employees or shareholders of a parent company, sometimes at a discount.

## Subscription and allotment

- The **subscription figure** (for example "135x") is total bids divided by shares on offer. Category-wise figures matter: very high QIB subscription is the strongest signal of institutional demand.
- When the retail portion is oversubscribed, allotment is by **lottery**, aiming to give at least one lot to as many applicants as possible. Applying for more lots does not raise your chances much.
- Applications are made through **ASBA**: money is blocked in your bank account via UPI (limit ₹5 lakh) and debited only if shares are allotted.

## Timeline

Since December 2023, listing happens on **T+3**, three working days after the issue closes. Allotment is finalised on T+1 and shares are credited on T+2.

## GMP (grey market premium)

GMP is an unofficial price at which IPO shares trade informally before listing. It is unregulated, easily manipulated and often wrong. SEBI does not recognise it, and SmartStock does not show it.

## SME IPOs

Small and medium enterprises list on NSE Emerge or BSE SME with smaller issue sizes, larger minimum investments and lighter disclosure. They carry higher risk and lower liquidity. SEBI has tightened SME IPO rules because of speculative excess.

## What to check before applying

1. Read the **Red Herring Prospectus (RHP)**: business, risks, use of proceeds, financials.
2. Fresh issue vs OFS: is the company raising money for growth, or are insiders exiting?
3. Valuation: compare the P/E at the upper price band with listed peers.
4. Profitability, debt and cash flow trends over three years.
5. Promoter background and any legal cases disclosed in the RHP.
6. Final-day category-wise subscription, especially QIB demand.

## In SmartStock

The IPO page shows open, upcoming and recently closed IPOs (closed in the last 30 days) from NSE's official data, with lot size, minimum investment, category-wise subscription and, for listed IPOs, the listing-day gain and current return.
