# What Moves the Indian Market

## Interest rates and the RBI

The RBI's Monetary Policy Committee sets the **repo rate** about every two months. Rate cuts lower borrowing costs and tend to help banks' loan growth, NBFCs, real estate, autos and the overall market; rate hikes do the opposite. Markets often move more on the RBI's commentary (stance) than on the decision itself, because the decision is usually expected.

## Inflation

CPI inflation above the RBI's 4% target (tolerance band 2% to 6%) makes rate cuts less likely. High input-cost inflation squeezes margins for FMCG, paints and autos.

## FII and DII flows

Foreign portfolio investors' net buying or selling can move the index sharply over days and weeks, and also affects the rupee. Domestic institutions, funded by monthly mutual fund SIPs, have increasingly absorbed FII selling.

## The rupee and crude oil

India imports most of its crude oil. Rising crude prices widen the trade deficit, weaken the rupee and raise costs for oil marketing companies, paints, tyres and airlines, while helping upstream producers. A weaker rupee helps exporters such as IT services and pharma.

## Global cues

US interest rates, the dollar, US tech stocks and Asian markets influence Indian markets, especially at the open. The SGX/GIFT NIFTY futures, traded before Indian markets open, indicate the likely opening direction.

## The Union Budget

Presented on 1 February. Tax changes (for example capital gains rates), government capital expenditure, fiscal deficit targets and sector-specific announcements can move whole sectors on the day.

## Earnings season

Quarterly results (mid-January, April, July, October onwards) drive individual stock moves. Results relative to analyst expectations matter more than the absolute numbers, and management guidance about the next quarters often matters most.

## Sector rotation

Money rotates between sectors with the economic cycle: banks and capital goods in an investment upcycle; FMCG and pharma as defensive havens in slowdowns; metals with global commodity prices; IT with US tech spending.
