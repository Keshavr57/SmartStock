# How the Indian Stock Market Works

Facts as of 2025.

## Exchanges, regulator and depositories

- **NSE** (National Stock Exchange) and **BSE** (BSE Ltd, formerly Bombay Stock Exchange) are India's main equity exchanges. NSE has most of the cash-market and almost all derivatives volume.
- **SEBI** (Securities and Exchange Board of India) regulates exchanges, brokers, mutual funds, listed companies and IPOs.
- **NSDL and CDSL** are the depositories that hold shares in electronic form. Your demat account with a broker is opened through one of them.
- **Clearing corporations** (NSE Clearing, Indian Clearing Corporation) guarantee settlement of every trade.

## Market hours (equity segment, Monday to Friday, IST)

- **Pre-open session: 9:00 to 9:15 AM.** Orders are collected from 9:00 to about 9:08 and an opening price is discovered by call auction.
- **Normal trading: 9:15 AM to 3:30 PM.**
- **Closing price**: the volume-weighted average price of the last 30 minutes (3:00 to 3:30 PM) on NSE.
- **Post-close session: 3:40 to 4:00 PM**, for trades at the closing price.

Exchanges are closed on weekends and on listed trading holidays (for example Diwali, Holi, Dussehra and national holidays). On Diwali a one-hour special "Muhurat trading" session is usually held in the evening.

## Settlement: T+1

Indian equities settle on **T+1**: shares bought on Monday are credited to the demat account on Tuesday, and the seller receives money the same day. India completed the move to T+1 in January 2023. An optional same-day (T+0) cycle was introduced for a limited set of stocks in 2024.

## Price bands and circuit breakers

- **Stock price bands**: most stocks outside the derivatives segment have daily price bands of 2%, 5%, 10% or 20%. A stock "hits the upper circuit" when it reaches the top of its band, and trading continues only within the band.
- **Index circuit breakers**: if the NIFTY 50 or SENSEX moves 10%, 15% or 20% from the previous close, trading halts market-wide. The halt length depends on the time of day, and a 20% move halts trading for the rest of the day.

## Indices

- **NIFTY 50**: 50 large companies on NSE, weighted by free-float market capitalisation.
- **SENSEX**: 30 large companies on BSE.
- **Sectoral indices** such as NIFTY BANK, NIFTY IT, NIFTY PHARMA and NIFTY FMCG track individual sectors.
- **India VIX** measures expected NIFTY volatility over the next 30 days, derived from option prices.

## Market capitalisation categories

SEBI's classification, used by mutual funds, ranks all listed companies by full market capitalisation:

- **Large cap**: companies ranked 1 to 100.
- **Mid cap**: ranked 101 to 250.
- **Small cap**: ranked 251 and below.

The list is updated every six months by AMFI.

## Who trades

- **FIIs / FPIs** (foreign portfolio investors): their daily net buying or selling often moves the index and the rupee.
- **DIIs** (domestic institutions such as mutual funds, insurers and pension funds): monthly SIP inflows have made them a large, steady buyer.
- **Retail investors**: individuals trading through brokers.
- **Promoters**: founders and controlling shareholders. Promoter holding and pledging are disclosed every quarter.
