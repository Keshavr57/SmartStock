# Mutual Funds, Index Funds and SIPs

## What a mutual fund is

A mutual fund pools money from many investors and invests it according to a stated mandate. You own units; the **NAV** (net asset value) per unit is published every business day. Funds are regulated by SEBI, and their categories are standardised so that a "large cap fund" must invest at least 80% in the top 100 companies.

## Main equity categories

- **Large cap, mid cap, small cap**: by company size; risk and volatility rise from large to small.
- **Flexi cap / multi cap**: invest across sizes.
- **Index funds and ETFs**: track an index such as NIFTY 50 at very low cost.
- **Sectoral and thematic funds**: concentrated in one theme; higher risk.
- **ELSS**: tax-saving funds with a 3-year lock-in (80C deduction under the old regime).

## Costs: expense ratio, direct vs regular

The **expense ratio** is deducted from the fund every year. **Direct plans** have no distributor commission and are cheaper than **regular plans**, often by 0.5 to 1 percentage point a year, which compounds significantly over decades. Index funds often cost 0.1% to 0.3%.

## Active vs passive

Most actively managed large-cap funds in India have trailed their benchmark over long periods after costs, which is why many investors use low-cost index funds for the large-cap portion of their portfolio.

## SIP (systematic investment plan)

A SIP invests a fixed amount every month. Benefits:
- **Discipline**: investing happens automatically.
- **Rupee cost averaging**: the same amount buys more units when prices are low and fewer when high, so the average cost is lower than the average price over the period.
- It removes the need to time the market.

Example: ₹5,000 a month for 15 years is ₹9 lakh invested. At an assumed 12% annual return it would grow to roughly ₹25 lakh; at 8% to about ₹17 lakh. Returns are not guaranteed, and equity funds can fall 30% or more in bad years.

## Stocks or funds?

Direct stock picking requires time to research and monitor companies. Funds offer diversification and professional management at a cost. Many investors combine a core of index funds with a smaller satellite of individual stocks they understand well.

## Taxes

Equity-oriented funds follow the same capital gains rules as listed shares: STCG 20% if held up to 12 months, LTCG 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh a year if held longer (rules effective 23 July 2024).
