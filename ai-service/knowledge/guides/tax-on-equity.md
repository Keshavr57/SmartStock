# Tax on Stocks and Equity Mutual Funds in India

Rules as of 2025 (after the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024, effective 23 July 2024). Tax law changes with each Budget, so confirm current rates on the Income Tax Department website or with a chartered accountant before filing.

## Short-term vs long-term

For listed equity shares and equity-oriented mutual funds (on which STT is paid), the holding period decides the type of gain:

- Held for **12 months or less**: short-term capital gain (STCG).
- Held for **more than 12 months**: long-term capital gain (LTCG).

The holding period runs from the date of purchase to the date of sale. Each lot is treated separately, on a first-in-first-out basis.

## Tax rates

- **STCG (Section 111A): 20%** of the gain (15% for sales before 23 July 2024).
- **LTCG (Section 112A): 12.5%** on gains **above ₹1.25 lakh** in a financial year (10% above ₹1 lakh for sales before 23 July 2024).
- Health and education cess of 4% applies on the tax, plus surcharge for high incomes.
- No indexation benefit is available on listed equity.

Example: you sell shares held for 18 months with a ₹2,00,000 gain and have no other equity LTCG that year. Taxable LTCG = ₹2,00,000 − ₹1,25,000 = ₹75,000. Tax = 12.5% × ₹75,000 = ₹9,375, plus cess.

## Grandfathering for shares bought before 1 February 2018

For equity bought on or before 31 January 2018, the cost of acquisition for LTCG is the higher of the actual cost and the fair market value (highest traded price) on 31 January 2018, but not more than the sale price. Gains made up to that date are protected from LTCG tax.

## Securities Transaction Tax (STT)

STT is charged on every trade on Indian exchanges, regardless of profit or loss:

- Delivery equity: 0.1% on both buy and sell.
- Intraday equity: 0.025% on the sell side.
- Futures: 0.02% of the sell-side value (from 1 October 2024).
- Options: 0.1% of the premium on the sell side (from 1 October 2024).

STT is not deductible when computing capital gains.

## Setting off and carrying forward losses

- **Short-term capital losses** can be set off against both short-term and long-term capital gains.
- **Long-term capital losses** can be set off only against long-term capital gains.
- Unabsorbed capital losses can be carried forward for **8 assessment years**, but only if the income tax return is filed by the due date.

**Tax-loss harvesting** means selling a losing position before the financial year ends (31 March) to book a loss that offsets gains. It changes the timing of tax, not the economics of the investment, and buying the same stock back immediately should be done with care.

## Dividends

Dividends are added to your income and taxed at your **slab rate**. Companies deduct TDS of 10% when dividends from that company exceed ₹10,000 in a financial year (the threshold was ₹5,000 until 31 March 2025). TDS is credited against your final tax.

## Intraday and F&O income

- **Intraday equity** profits are treated as speculative business income, taxed at slab rates.
- **Futures and options** profits are non-speculative business income, taxed at slab rates. Expenses such as brokerage can be deducted, and a tax audit may apply above certain turnover thresholds.

## ELSS and Section 80C

Equity Linked Savings Schemes (ELSS) qualify for a deduction of up to ₹1.5 lakh under Section 80C, but **only under the old tax regime**. They have a 3-year lock-in, the shortest among 80C options. Gains on redemption are taxed as LTCG under Section 112A.

## In SmartStock

SmartStock is paper trading: no tax applies to virtual profits. The 0.1% charge on each SmartStock order is a simplified brokerage-plus-charges figure, not an actual tax.
