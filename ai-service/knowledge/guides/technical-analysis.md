# Technical Analysis Basics

Technical analysis studies price and volume to judge trend and momentum. It describes what the market is doing; it does not explain why, and no indicator predicts the future reliably. Use it together with fundamentals and risk management.

## Trend and moving averages

- A **simple moving average (SMA)** is the average closing price over N days. The 50-day and 200-day SMAs are the most watched.
- Price above a rising 200-day SMA is generally considered a long-term uptrend; below a falling one, a downtrend.
- A **golden cross** is the 50-day moving above the 200-day; a **death cross** is the reverse. Both lag price and give false signals in sideways markets.
- The **exponential moving average (EMA)** weights recent prices more and reacts faster.

## Support and resistance

- **Support** is a price zone where buying has repeatedly stopped declines; **resistance** is where selling has capped rallies.
- A breakout above resistance on high volume is considered more reliable than one on low volume.
- Once broken, resistance often becomes support and vice versa.

## RSI (Relative Strength Index)

RSI (14-day by default) ranges from 0 to 100 and measures the speed of recent price changes.
- Above 70 is called **overbought**; below 30, **oversold**.
- In strong trends RSI can stay above 70 or below 30 for long periods, so these levels are not automatic sell or buy signals.
- **Divergence** (price makes a new high while RSI does not) can warn that momentum is fading.

## MACD

MACD = 12-day EMA − 26-day EMA, with a 9-day EMA of MACD as the signal line. A MACD crossing above its signal line is read as improving momentum. It lags price and works poorly in range-bound markets.

## Volume

Volume confirms moves: a rally on rising volume shows participation; a rally on falling volume is weaker. Unusually high volume often accompanies news, results or block deals.

## Candlesticks

Each candle shows open, high, low and close for a period. Green (or hollow) candles close above the open; red candles close below.
- **Doji**: open and close almost equal, indecision.
- **Hammer**: long lower wick after a decline, possible buying interest.
- **Engulfing**: a candle whose body fully covers the previous one, possible reversal.
Single patterns have weak predictive power on their own.

## 52-week high and low

The highest and lowest prices of the past year. Stocks near 52-week highs are showing strength; near lows, weakness. Neither alone means cheap or expensive.

## Limits of technical analysis

- Indicators are derived from past prices, so they lag.
- Backtested strategies often fail live because of costs, slippage and overfitting.
- News, results and macro events can override any chart pattern.
